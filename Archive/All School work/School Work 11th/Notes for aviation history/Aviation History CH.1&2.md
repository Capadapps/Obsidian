# ✈️ Eras in Aviation

---

## 🕰️ Early Years (200 BC – 1890 AD)

**People**

- **Greek Mythology**: Daedalus & Icarus (Island of Crete)
- **Roger Bacon**: Franciscan monk; worked with optics, flying machines, and submarines
- **Leonardo da Vinci**: Anticipated the parachute, helicopter, and fixed-wing aircraft
- **Francesco de Lana**: Jesuit priest; interested in lighter-than-air flight; also associated with early tactile reading concepts
- **Montgolfier Brothers (1780s)**
    - First hot air balloon flight (1782, France)
    - First aeronauts: duck, sheep, rooster
    - First manned flight (Nov 1783)
- **Sir George Cayley (1773–1857)**: Invented an early wind tunnel; foundational aerodynamics toward heavier-than-air flight
- **William Henson**: Early monoplane concepts; steam engine with propeller
- **Aeronautical Society (1866)**
- **U.S. Civil War (1862–1865)**: Military use of balloons

**Inventions**

- Gas balloons
- Hot air balloons

**Concepts**

- Parachute
- Helicopter (concept)
- Human-powered glider (concept)

---

## 🪂 Pre‑Powered Flight (1860 – 1903)

**People**

- **Otto Lilienthal (1848–1896)**: German engineer; pioneer in glider flight; Berlin’s airport once named after him
- **Octave Chanute (1832–1910)**: Popularized and tested biplane gliders; mentor to the Wrights
- **Capt. Ferdinand Ferber**: French Army officer; experimented with gliders; killed in a low‑altitude turn
- **Samuel Langley**: Astronomer and Smithsonian Secretary; model powered flights, human‑carrying attempts

**Inventions / Concepts**

- Rigid‑frame aircraft
- Human‑controlled flight
- Weight‑shift control
- Wing design and lifting surface refinement

---

## 🛩️ Early Powered Flight (1903 – WWI)

**Key Event**

- **Wright Brothers**: First controlled, sustained, powered flight at Kitty Hawk, NC (Dec 17, 1903)
    - Developed launch/rail system
    - Pursued patents leading to litigation until government intervention
    - **Wilbur**: Died in 1912
    - **Orville**: Continued aviation work and advocacy

**People**

- **Samuel Pierpont Langley**: Competing powered flight attempts
- **Wright Brothers**
- **Glenn Curtiss**
    - Advanced ailerons (vs. wing‑warping)
    - Curtiss Aeroplane & Motor Co.
    - Built engines for balloons; later aircraft manufacturer
    - Designed JN‑4 “Jenny” military trainer
    - Mentored **Theodore Ellyson** (U.S. Naval Aviator #1)
    - Founded many aviation corporations; died at 52 (appendicitis)
- **Glenn L. Martin**: Early manufacturer and designer
- **Louis Blériot**: First to fly across the English Channel (1909)
- **Calbraith Perry “Cal” Rodgers**: First U.S. transcontinental flight (1911)
- **Lincoln Beachey**: Famous stunt pilot

**Notes**

- **Count Ferdinand von Zeppelin** created the rigid airship (Zeppelin)
- The Aeronautical Society of Great Britain discussed power‑to‑weight challenges and funding for models
- Zeppelins used for bombing; later speed improvements. **Igor Sikorsky** advanced large aircraft and later flying boats for Pan Am

## ⚔️ World War I (1914 – 1918)

*(Add detail: Sopwith Camel, Fokker designs, interrupter gear, rapid tech advances)*

**Roles and Tactics**

- Aircraft used as **reconnaissance**, **fighters**, **bombers**, and for **artillery spotting**
- Early aircraft considered non‑combatant; pilots initially used pistols and rifles → rapid adoption of machine guns and synchronization gear
- Missions were extremely dangerous; high casualty rates

**Industry and Output**

- **Germany** targeted ~1,000 aircraft per month; by war’s end ~30,000 per year
- **Britain** had highly skilled manufacturing capacity

**Key Aircraft and Figures**

- **French SPAD XIII**: Major Allied fighter
- **Eddie Rickenbacker** (U.S. ace), “Hat in the Ring” Squadron
- Planes to note: RAF R.E.8, SE‑5a, Bristol F.2B, Fokker E.III, Fokker Dr.I

**The Red Baron**

- **Manfred von Richthofen**: Top WWI ace with 80 confirmed victories; shot down in 1918

**Other Notes**

- Bombers used against London; e.g., Gotha raids. Handley Page bombers on the Western Front
- Zeppelins used for strategic bombing at significant material cost

**Technology Trends**

- More powerful engines
- Air‑cooled and water‑cooled designs
- Multi‑engine aircraft, improving range and payload
- Increasingly advanced armament
- Typical pilot age ~19; life expectancy on some missions was very low

## 🏆 The Golden Age (1918 – 1939)

*(Add detail: records, commercial growth, air races, navigation aids)*

**Context**

- WWI ended Nov 11, 1918 (Armistice Day)
- 1939: Start of WWII; late 1930s rise of Hitler
- Immediate post‑war recession, then aviation boom

**Civil Aviation Growth**

- Barnstorming: Surplus trainers sold to pilots offering rides and aerobatics
- No federal airworthiness regulations until 1927 → many accidents
- Rapid rise of commercial aviation and luxury air travel
- Sleek, all‑metal monoplanes; advances in speed, range, and avionics
- Growth drivers: surplus aircraft, many trained pilots, strong public interest, beginnings of general air transport

**Records and Notables**

- **Charles Lindbergh**: Spirit of St. Louis, first solo nonstop New York–Paris flight (33½ hours); famous ticker‑tape parade; airmail pilot and test pilot
- **Amelia Earhart**: Women’s altitude record, multiple distance records; began flying in 1921; disappeared during world flight attempt
- **Douglas “Wrong‑Way” Corrigan**: Intended transcontinental, flew to Ireland in 1938
- **Wiley Post**: Lost sight in one eye; flew around the world with a navigator, then solo; pioneered high‑altitude flight with a pressure suit; identified the jet stream; died with Will Rogers in 1935
- **James “Jimmy” Doolittle**: Army‑trained pilot; won Bendix and Thompson trophies; helped develop high‑octane fuel; first blind/IFR takeoff and landing (1929); led the Doolittle Raid (B‑25s) in 1942
- 1924: First around‑the‑world flight by U.S. Army Douglas World Cruisers

**Policy and Navigation**

- 1925 Air Mail (Kelly) Act: Established contract airmail and weight‑based rates
- Airlines often needed day‑and‑night operations to be profitable
- Federal airway beacons and ground‑painted arrow markers aided navigation
