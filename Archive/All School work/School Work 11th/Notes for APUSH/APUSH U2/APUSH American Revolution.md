### Legacies of the American Revolution

- **Slavery**
    - In the North: Increasing public questioning of slavery as conflicting with American values. Many states passed gradual emancipation laws, sometimes with compensation to enslavers.
    - In the South: Slavery became more entrenched and expanded.
- **Social Structure Changes**
    - Breakdown of rigid social class systems.
    - Southern states abolished primogeniture laws after the Revolution.
    - All state legislatures banned aristocratic titles.
- **Religious Freedom**
    - Virginia Statute for Religious Freedom, authored by Thomas Jefferson, advanced the separation of church and state.
    - The Anglican Church was disestablished in Virginia.
- **Women in the Revolution**
    - Roles and public perceptions of women began to change as they contributed to the war effort and civic life.
    - "Molly Pitcher" became a symbolic name for women who brought water and supplies to front lines.
    - Nancy Morgan Hart reportedly captured and shot British soldiers after serving them food.
    - Abigail Adams was an influential voice in the early republic and urged John Adams to consider greater rights and opportunities for women.
- **Republican Ideals**
    - Republicanism, popular sovereignty, representative government, consent of the governed, limited government power, and individual liberty.
    - Belief that strong families and civic virtue are essential for a strong republic.
- **George Washington**
    - Model of republican leadership and served two presidential terms.
    - Manumitted enslaved people in his will, recognizing the hypocrisy of slavery.
    - Idealized agrarian civic virtue and was called the "American Cincinnatus" for giving up power rather than seizing it.
- **Global Impact**
    - The ideas of the Revolution, articulated in the Declaration of Independence, reverberated globally.
    - Inspired independence and reform movements in France, Haiti, Latin America, and beyond.
