### **VOCABULARY**

**Railroad Era** – produced a continental network of railroads that could move the products of a new large-scale industries

**Last Frontier** – the settlement of lands between the Mississippi and Pacific Ocean

**Gilded Age** – a time during which the captains of industry controlled large corporations, created great fortunes, and dominated politics, while the problems of farmlands and burgeoning cities festered under the surface

**Laissez-faire capitalism:** the idea of government non-regulation of business. Allows industries to flourish

**Social Darwinism** – theory of natural selection, bolstered views of economic conservatives. Natural selection and survival of the fittest should be applied to the marketplace. Some believed concentrating wealth in the hands of the “fit” benefited everyone.

**Gospel of Wealth** – wealthy had a God-given responsibility to carry out projects of civic philanthropy for the benefit of society

**Struggle of Organized labor** – lockouts, blacklists, yellow-dog contracts, private guards, court injunctions

**Frontier hypothesis:** Frederick Jackson Turner’s “The Significance of the Frontier in American History” argued that 300 years of frontier experience shaped American culture by promoting independence and individualism

**Dawes Act (1887)** – designed to break up tribal organizations, which many felt kept American Indians from becoming “civilized.”

**Plessy v. Ferguson (1896)** – upheld separate but equal accommodations, supported a wave of segregationist laws commonly known as Jim Crow

**Booker T. Washington** – advocated for accommodation and acceptance of treatment, education and vocational training for African Americans will earn them respect

**National Grange Movement** – dedicated to the social and economic uplift of farmers

**Interstate Commerce Act (1886)** – regulated railroad rates, led to Interstate Commerce Commission (ICC), the first regulatory industry – stabilized rates and curtailed destructive competition

**Sherman Anti-Trust Act** – outlawing “combinations in restraint of trade”

**Election of 1896** – McKinley defeats William Jennings Bryan, illustrates the nation’s interest in industry and growth
