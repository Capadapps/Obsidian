**Sections:**

1. Background to Revolution

---

## 🌟 **Section 1: Background to Revolution**

### **Focus Question:**

What were the factors leading to the revolutions of the late eighteenth century?

### **Key Causes of Revolution**

6. **Long-Term Causes**
    - Erosion of state authority due to economic and social changes.
    - Enlightenment ideas challenging tradition (liberty, equality, sovereignty).
    - Financial crises from expensive imperial wars (e.g., Seven Years’ War).
7. **Social Change**
    - **Nobility/Clergy:** Held privileges like tax exemptions, titles, and access to land.
    - **Middle Class:** Gained wealth through commerce but lacked noble status.
    - **Peasantry:** Bore the heaviest tax burdens, fueling resentment.
8. **Enlightenment Contradictions**
    - **Slavery vs. Enlightenment Ideals:** Enlightenment argued equality and liberty, yet slavery persisted in colonies.
9. **Growing Demands**
    - **Liberty:** Individual rights, free speech, and self-governance.
    - **Equality:** Legal equality (ambiguous as race and gender inequalities remained).

## 🗽 **Section 2: The American Revolutionary Era**

### **Focus Question:**

Why and how did American colonists forge a new, independent nation?

### **Key Events Leading to Independence**

10. **British Policies After the Seven Years' War**
    - Stamp Act (1765): Direct taxation angered colonists.
    - Proclamation Line: Restricted westward expansion.
11. **Colonial Grievances**
    - **"No taxation without representation"** became the rallying cry.
    - **Monopoly on Tea:** Sparked rebellion (Boston Tea Party, 1773).
12. **War for Independence**
    - **France's Aid:** Financial and military support pivotal for American victory.
    - **Declaration of Independence (1776):** Universalized traditional rights.
13. **Framing the Constitution**
    - Established a federal government with checks and balances.
    - **Compromise:** Anti-Federalists gained a Bill of Rights.
14. **Limitations**
    - Liberty for whites, inequality for women, enslaved Africans, and Indigenous people.

## 🇫🇷 **Section 3: Revolution in France**

### **Focus Question:**

How did the events of 1789 result in a constitutional monarchy in France, and what were the consequences?

### **Key Causes of the French Revolution**

15. **Financial Crisis**
    - France’s debts skyrocketed (Seven Years’ War, American Revolution).
    - **Louis XVI** failed to implement reforms effectively.
16. **The Estates General**
    - Representatives from the three estates gathered.
    - **Third Estate Grievances:** Called for representation and reform.
17. **Popular Uprising**
    - **Storming of the Bastille (July 14, 1789):** Symbol of royal oppression.
    - **The Great Fear:** Peasant revolts aimed to destroy feudal obligations.
    - **Women's March on Versailles:** Forced Louis XVI to return to Paris.
18. **Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen**
    - Guaranteed liberty, property, and resistance to oppression.
19. **Constitutional Monarchy**
    - The king remained head of state but shared power with the National Assembly.

## ⚔️ **Section 4: World War and Republican France**

### **Focus Question:**

Why and how did the French Revolution take a radical turn entailing terror at home and war with European powers?

### **Radicalization of the Revolution**

20. **The Jacobins and Sans-Culottes**
    - **Jacobins:** Radical political club; supported violence for revolutionary goals.
    - **Sans-Culottes:** Working-class revolutionaries demanding bread and rights.
21. **The September Massacres**
    - Violence erupted; counter-revolutionaries were executed.
22. **The Committee of Public Safety**
    - **Robespierre’s Reign of Terror (1793-1794):** Enforced loyalty to the Republic through mass executions.
    - Reorganized the economy and strengthened the military.
23. **The Thermidorian Reaction**
    - Robespierre executed (1794); radical policies reversed.
    - The **Directory** took over but failed to gain popular support.

## 🏛️ **Section 5: The Napoleonic Era (1799–1815)**

### **Focus Question:**

How did Napoleon Bonaparte assume control of France and much of Europe, and what factors led to his downfall?

### **Napoleon’s Rise to Power**

24. **Coup of 1799**
    - Napoleon overthrew the Directory; became **First Consul**.
25. **Domestic Reforms**
    - **Napoleonic Code:** Secured property rights, equality before the law (but limited women’s rights).
    - **Central Bank:** Stabilized the economy.
    - **Concordat of 1801:** Reestablished Catholicism in France.
26. **Expansion and Empire**
    - Created the **Grand Empire** with satellite states.
    - **Continental System:** Economic blockade of Britain (ultimately failed).
27. **Napoleon’s Downfall**
    - **Invasion of Russia (1812):** “Scorched earth” strategy led to Napoleon’s defeat.
    - **Coalition Forces:** European powers united to defeat Napoleon.
    - **Exile to Elba:** Napoleon abdicated (1814), returned briefly during the **Hundred Days** but was defeated at **Waterloo (1815)**.
    - Final exile to **Saint Helena**, where he died in 1821.

## 🔍 **Key Terms and Concepts**

28. **Enlightenment Ideas:** Liberty, equality, sovereignty.
29. **Estates General:** Three estates of France (clergy, nobility, commoners).
30. **Reign of Terror:** Robespierre's radical enforcement of revolutionary ideals.
31. **Napoleonic Code:** Legal framework under Napoleon.
32. **Continental System:** Economic warfare against Britain.
