## **The Congress of Vienna and the Balance of Power**

### *How was peace restored and maintained after 1815?*

After Napoleon’s defeat in 1814, the **Quadruple Alliance** (Russia, Prussia, Austria, and Great Britain) met at the **Congress of Vienna** to establish a long-lasting peace in Europe.

### **Key Goals of the Congress of Vienna**

1. **Balance of Power** – Prevent any one nation from dominating Europe again.
2. **Redrawing Boundaries** – Territories previously under French control were redistributed.
3. **Conservative Restoration** – Monarchs and traditional governments were reinstated.

### **Atmosphere at the Congress**

- The congressmen were serious about creating a stable and lasting order for Europe.
- However, the atmosphere in Vienna was celebratory due to Napoleon's defeat and the end of revolutionary upheaval.

### **How Diplomacy Was Conducted**

- Diplomacy was led by key figures such as:
    - **Klemens von Metternich** (Austria)
    - **Robert Castlereagh** (Great Britain)
    - **Charles Talleyrand** (France)
- The goal was to **discourage aggression** and prevent another European power from rising to supremacy.

### **What Was the Balance of Power?**

- A system where power was distributed across nations to prevent dominance by any one state.
- This ensured **stability** and **discouraged wars** for territorial expansion.

### **Why Did the Congress Believe This Would Work?**

- It **equalized** states, reducing incentives for war.
- Strengthened certain states (e.g., Prussia’s expansion as a “sentinel on the Rhine”) to counterbalance France.

## **The Concert of Europe and the Congress System**

- **Napoleon briefly returned to power in 1815** but was decisively defeated at **Waterloo**.
- The **Quadruple Alliance** agreed to meet periodically to discuss maintaining peace.
- This led to the creation of the **Congress System**, where major powers worked together to prevent conflicts.

### **The Concert of Europe**

- A **loose agreement** between major European powers to maintain the **balance of power**, **political stability**, and **territorial boundaries**.
- Although not always united, it provided a period of **relative peace** after decades of war.

### **Why Was This a Turning Point in Diplomacy?**

- First long-term application of the **balance of power**.
- Introduced **multilateral diplomacy**, where multiple nations worked together to resolve disputes.

---

## **Metternich and Conservatism**

### **Who Was Klemens von Metternich?**

- Austrian Foreign Minister (1809–1848).
- A staunch **conservative** who sought to maintain **monarchical rule** and suppress revolutionary ideas.

### **Why Did Metternich Support Authoritarianism?**

- He believed **human nature was erratic** and **prone to chaos**, requiring **strong rule** to maintain order.

### **The Holy Alliance (1815)**

- Formed by **Austria, Prussia, and Russia** to:
    - Suppress **liberal and nationalist movements**.
    - Stop **revolutions** before they could gain momentum.
- **France and Britain did not join** since France was still transitioning from its revolution and Britain was more liberal.

### **How Did the Concert of Europe Handle Revolutions?**

- **Spain and Italy (1820)**: France, under the Concert of Europe’s approval, **sent troops to Spain** to crush liberal uprisings and restore the monarchy.

### **The Karlsbad Decrees (1819)**

- Enforced in **German states** to:
    - Ban **liberal political groups**.
    - **Censor universities and newspapers**.
    - Establish **a network of spies** to suppress dissent.

### **How Did Russia Respond to Liberal Protests (1825)?**

- **Sent troops** to crush liberal revolts, reinforcing conservative rule.

## **Liberalism and the Middle Class**

### **Definition of Liberalism**

- A political ideology emphasizing individual liberties, limited government, free markets, and representative democracy.

### **Liberalism in Europe (1815)**

- Only **France** and **Great Britain** had achieved partial liberal reforms.
- Liberalism was still struggling against **conservative opposition** but had gained support from:
    - **Wealthy industrial and commercial elites**
    - **Business elites advocating laissez-faire policies**

### **Liberal Demands from Government**

- **Representative government**
- **Protection of individual liberties**
- **Economic freedom** (laissez-faire policies)

### **Key Liberties and Freedoms Sought by Liberals**

- **Freedom of speech**
- **Freedom of the press**
- **Freedom of assembly**
- **Religious freedom**
- **Property rights**

### **Liberal Economic Policies**

- Strong **support for free markets** (laissez-faire).
- **Minimal government interference** in the economy.
- **Opposition to labor unions**, which were seen as threats to business interests.

### **Why Were Unions Outlawed?**

- Business elites and governments saw unions as:
    - A **threat to profits**
    - A **challenge to employer control**
    - A **source of social instability**

### **Democracy and Property Qualifications**

- Early liberals **favored representative government** but restricted voting rights to **property owners**.
- **Reason:** They believed political power should be **limited to the educated and wealthy**.

### **Radical Republicanism vs. Liberalism**

- **Radical Republicans wanted:**
    - **Universal male suffrage** (voting rights for all men)
    - **More direct democracy**
    - **Stronger social and economic reforms**

---

## **The Growing Appeal of Nationalism**

### **Definition of Nationalism**

- The belief that people with a **common language, history, culture, and territory** should form their own independent nation.

### **Factors Driving Nationalism**

- **Napoleonic Wars** inspired unity and national identity.
- **Desire to defend the "fatherland"** (especially in German-speaking Europe).

### **Obstacles to Nationalist Movements**

- **Multiethnic empires (Austrian, Russian, Ottoman) refused independence to national minorities.**
- **Existing borders conflicted with nationalist aspirations.**

### **What Facilitated the Growth of Nationalism?**

4. **Industrialization** – Created a stronger national economy and common interests.
5. **Urbanization** – Brought people together in cities, fostering a shared identity.

### **How Did Nationalism Cause Disintegration in Multiethnic States?**

- Ethnic groups under imperial rule **demanded self-determination**.
- **Austrian, Russian, and Ottoman Empires faced internal revolts.**
- **Italy and Germany pushed for unification**, seeing current borders as outdated.

### **Why Did Nationalism Depend on Industrial and Urban Societies?**

6. **Mass communication and transportation unified people across regions.**
7. **Industrial economies required a cohesive national identity for economic efficiency.**

### **Liberalism and Nationalism: How They Worked Together**

- Both **opposed absolute monarchy** and **supported representative government**.
- **Liberalism promoted individual freedoms**, while **nationalism promoted collective identity**.
- **Shared enemy: Conservative monarchies and empires.**

---

## **The Foundations of Modern Socialism**

### **Early Socialist Thought**

- **Reaction to the French Revolution, industrialization, and laissez-faire policies.**
- Socialists **criticized capitalism** for creating:
    - **Selfish individualism**
    - **Economic inequality**
    - **Fragmentation of communities**
- **Goal:** Economic equality through **government regulation** or **collective ownership**.

### **Key Socialist Thinkers and Their Ideas**

### **Count Henri de Saint-Simon**

- Society should be **led by scientists and industrialists**, not aristocrats.
- Advocated for **state-planned economies** to benefit all.

### **Charles Fourier**

- Called for **self-sufficient communities** (phalansteries).
- Advocated for **gender equality** and **worker rights**.

### **Robert Owen**

- **Factory owner who improved worker conditions** (New Lanark, Scotland).
- Supported **education and social reforms**.

### **Pierre-Joseph Proudhon**

- Argued that **"property is theft"** – wealth should be shared.
- Supported **mutualist economic systems**.

### **Louis Blanc**

- Proposed **state-funded workshops** to provide jobs.
- **Believed the government should guarantee employment**.

### **Why Did Workers Embrace Socialism?**

- **Industrialization led to exploitation and poor working conditions.**
- **Laissez-faire laws prevented workers from organizing in unions.**
- **French urban workers in the 1830s and 1840s became radicalized.**

### **Why Was the Rise of Workers in France a Threat?**

- **Socialist uprisings challenged the monarchy and existing power structures.**
- **Governments feared revolutions and loss of control over industries.**

---

## **The Birth of Marxist Socialism**

### **Karl Marx and His Contributions**

- Developed **Marxist socialism** (Communism).
- Argued that **history is a class struggle** between:
    - **Bourgeoisie (owners)** vs. **Proletariat (workers)**.
- Advocated for **a proletarian revolution to overthrow capitalism**.

### **How Did Marx Develop His Ideas?**

- **Studied philosophy in Berlin** and engaged with radical thinkers.
- **Turned to journalism** and was forced to flee Prussia.
- **Relied on Friedrich Engels** for financial support.
- **Settled in London after the 1848 revolutions**, where he wrote extensively on socialism.

### **Marx’s Argument for Communism**

- **Capitalism exploits workers** and will lead to its own downfall.
- **The proletariat must rise up** and **seize the means of production**.
- **A classless, stateless society (Communism) will emerge** after capitalism is abolished.

---

## **Conclusion**

- **Liberalism promoted individual freedoms but was limited to the elite.**
- **Nationalism encouraged unity but also disintegration of empires.**
- **Socialism and Marxism emerged as a response to capitalism, calling for economic equality.**

## **The Tenets of Romanticism**

- The **Romantic Movement** peaked between **1790 and the 1840s**, reacting against the **rationality, order, and restraint** of the **Enlightenment**.
- Romantics emphasized:
    - **Emotional intensity** and **exuberance**
    - **Unrestrained imagination**
    - **Spontaneity in art and personal life**
- Their works explored deep emotions such as:
    - **Love**
    - **Desire**
    - **Despair**
- **Opposed secularization** – sought religious inspiration and delved into **supernatural themes** rather than civic engagement.
- **Romanticism as a Lifestyle**: Many **lived dramatically**, experiencing:
    - **Obsessive love affairs**
    - **Duels**
    - **Madness and illness**
    - **Suicide**
- **Individualism**: Romantics believed in **self-expression and personal fulfillment** as the ultimate purpose in life.

---

## **Romanticism and Its Views on Nature & History**

### **Romantic Views on Nature**

- Nature was seen as a **source of spiritual renewal, beauty, and truth**.
- Often depicted as **a living force deeply connected to the human soul**.

### **Romantic Views on History**

- **Idealized the past**, particularly the **Middle Ages**, for its perceived authenticity, chivalry, and heroism.

### **Romanticism and Nationalism**

- **Supported nationalism** by emphasizing:
    - **Cultural heritage**
    - **Folklore and mythology**
    - **National languages and traditions**

---

## **Romantic Literature**

### **Key Romantic Writers & Their Works**

### **William Wordsworth**

- **English Romantic poet** who, alongside **Samuel Taylor Coleridge**, helped launch the **Romantic Age**.
- **Focused on nature, emotions, and the beauty of ordinary life.**

### **Samuel Taylor Coleridge**

- **English poet, literary critic, philosopher, and theologian.**
- **Key figure in the Romantic Movement** and a member of the **Lake Poets**.
- **Known for "The Rime of the Ancient Mariner" and "Kubla Khan."**

### **Germain de Staël**

- **Philosopher, writer, and political theorist** in **French and Swiss intellectual circles**.
- **Promoted Romanticism in France and opposed Napoleon.**

### **Victor Hugo**

- **French Romantic writer** known for **"Les Misérables" and "The Hunchback of Notre-Dame."**
- **Emphasized strong emotions, historical themes, and social justice.**

### **Jacob and Wilhelm Grimm (Brothers Grimm)**

- **German folklorists** who collected and published **traditional folk tales.**
- Their works helped **preserve German cultural identity** and inspired nationalism.

---

## **Romanticism in Art & Music**

### **Romantic Artists & Their Contributions**

### **Eugène Delacroix**

- **French Romantic painter** known for **dramatic compositions and bold colors.**
- **Often depicted historical, mythological, and exotic subjects.**
- **Famous Work**: *Liberty Leading the People*.

### **Caspar David Friedrich**

- **German Romantic painter** focused on **landscapes and themes of solitude.**
- **Depicted nature as vast, mysterious, and deeply emotional.**
- **Famous Work**: *Wanderer Above the Sea of Fog*.

### **Joseph M.W. Turner**

- **British Romantic painter** known for **dramatic landscapes and seascapes.**
- **Used expressive, almost abstract brushwork** to capture the power of nature.
- **Famous Work**: *The Fighting Temeraire*.

### **John Constable**

- **English Romantic painter** celebrated for **landscapes of the English countryside.**
- **Focused on natural beauty and rural life.**
- **Famous Work**: *The Hay Wain*.

---

## **Romantic Music**

- Romantic composers **abandoned classical structures** in favor of **emotionally powerful and expressive music**.
- Used a **wide range of musical forms** to create vivid and dramatic soundscapes.

### **Ludwig van Beethoven – The Most Famous Romantic Composer**

- **Bridged the Classical and Romantic eras.**
- **Revolutionized music** with:
    - **Emotional depth**
    - **Bold innovations**
    - **Expansive orchestration**
- **Iconic Works:**
    - **Ninth Symphony ("Ode to Joy")**
    - **Moonlight Sonata**
    - **Fifth Symphony**
    - **Eroica Symphony**
- **Expressed intense personal emotions**, from despair to triumph.
