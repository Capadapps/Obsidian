**AP European History: Chapter 20 Notes**

**Section 1: The Industrial Revolution in Britain (1780-1850)**

**Focus Question:** Identify the origins of the Industrial Revolution in Britain and explain how it developed between 1780 and 1850.

**Why Britain?**

1.	**Scientific and Enlightenment Influence:**

•	Fostered a culture of progress, research, and experimentation.

•	Institutions like the **British Royal Society of Arts** incentivized innovation.

2.	**Colonial Possessions:**

•	Provided raw materials (e.g., cotton) and markets, fueling economic growth.

3.	**Agricultural Advancements:**

•	Increased food production through crop rotation and selective breeding.

•	Freed labor from farms, creating a surplus workforce.

4.	**Economic and Social Factors:**

•	**High wages** encouraged innovation to increase productivity.

•	Rising family incomes created higher demand for manufactured goods.

5.	**Natural Resources and Infrastructure:**

•	Abundant **coal** and **iron deposits** in England and Wales.

•	A **canal-building boom** improved transportation of materials.

•	Supportive government policies facilitated industrial growth.

**Key Developments:**

•	**Industrial Growth Rates:** Accelerated from **0.7% annually (1700-1760)** to **3% (1801-1831)**.

•	**Technological Innovation:** Early factories organized labor around **highly efficient machines**, revolutionizing production.

**Section 2: Technological Innovations and Early Factories**

**Focus Question:** How did technological advancements shape production and labor in Britain?

**Textile Industry Innovations:**

1.	**Imports from India:**

•	Inspired British manufacturers to compete with high-quality Indian textiles.

2.	**Major Inventions in Britain:**

•	**James Hargreaves:** Invented the **spinning jenny** (1765), increasing thread production.

•	**Richard Arkwright:** Developed the **water frame**, using water power for spinning.

•	**Samuel Crompton:** Combined spinning jenny and water frame into the **spinning mule**.

•	**Edmund Cartwright:** Invented the **power loom**, mechanizing weaving.

3.	**Child Labor:**

•	Factories relied heavily on child workers due to their low cost and societal acceptance.

**The Steam Engine Breakthrough:**

1.	**Shift to Coal Energy:**

•	Wood shortages and rising demand for power led Britain to rely on **coal**.

2.	**Thomas Savery and Thomas Newcomen:**

•	Early steam engines developed to pump water from mines.

3.	**James Watt’s Improvements:**

•	Made steam engines more efficient and adaptable for various industries.

4.	**Impact of Steam Power:**

•	Revolutionized mining, textiles, and transportation, creating steam-powered ships and trains.

**Section 3: New Patterns of Working and Living**

**Focus Question:** How did work evolve during the Industrial Revolution, and how did it change daily life for workers?

**Work in Early Factories:**

1.	**The Factory System:**

•	Workers faced strict discipline, long hours, and harsh conditions.

•	Early factories resembled poorhouses, deterring many cottage workers.

2.	**Child Labor:**

•	Factory owners employed children for their low wages and docility.

•	Reforms like the **Factory Acts** (beginning in 1802) gradually limited child labor.

3.	**Sexual Division of Labor:**

•	**“Separate Spheres”** emerged:

•	Men as primary wage earners.

•	Women focused on child care and domestic work.

4.	**Living Standards:**

•	**1792-1815:** Wages and living standards declined due to wars with France.

•	Improvements in living standards were not widely felt until the mid-1800s.

**Section 4: Relations Between Capital and Labor**

**Focus Question:** How did the changes brought by the Industrial Revolution lead to new social classes, and how did people respond to this new structure?

**The New Social Classes:**

1.	**Bourgeoisie (Middle Class):**

•	Factory owners and industrialists driven by competition and profit.

•	Often newly wealthy and proud of their success.

2.	**Proletariat (Working Class):**

•	Wage laborers in factories, mines, and urban centers.

3.	**Criticism of Industrial Society:**

•	**William Blake:** Denounced factories as “satanic mills.”

•	**Friedrich Engels:** Highlighted exploitation and poor conditions in *The Condition of the Working Class in England*.

**Worker Responses:**

1.	**Luddites:**

•	Destroyed machinery they believed threatened their livelihoods.

2.	**Labor Movements:**

•	**Combination Acts (1799):** Initially banned unions but repealed in 1824.

•	**Robert Owen:** Advocated for better conditions and organized cooperative communities.

3.	**Chartist Movement:**

•	Pushed for political reforms, including universal male suffrage and fair wages.

**The Role of Slavery:**

•	Profits from colonial plantations and the slave trade contributed to Britain’s wealth.

•	However, historians debate whether these profits directly funded industrial development.

**Long-Term Impacts of the Industrial Revolution:**

•	Transformed global economies and societies, giving rise to **capitalism** and **consumerism**.

•	Urbanization reshaped living patterns, while innovations in steam power and factory systems laid the groundwork for modern industry.
