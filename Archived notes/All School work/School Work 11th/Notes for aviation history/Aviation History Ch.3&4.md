# ✈️ Aviation History Notes (Interwar to WWII)

---

## **Air Mail (1920s)**

- Many air mail pilots died due to poor weather and navigation challenges.
- **1925 Air Mail Act (Kelly Act):** Allowed private companies to carry U.S. mail, leading to the growth of early airlines.

---

## **Record Flights & Aviation Milestones**

### **1927**

- **May 21, 1927:** Charles Lindbergh — *first nonstop solo flight from New York to Paris* in *Spirit of St. Louis* (33.5 hours).
- **Amelia Earhart:** First woman to fly solo across the Atlantic; set multiple altitude and distance records.

### **Famous Aircraft**

- **P-38 Lightning:** Dual-engine, long-range WWII fighter.
- **F4U Corsair:** Advanced carrier-based WWII fighter with distinctive gull wings.
- **Douglas World Cruiser:** Completed first around-the-world flight (Lt. Cmdr. Reed, 1924).
- **Lockheed Vega:** Record-setting aircraft flown by Wiley Post and Amelia Earhart.
- **Ford Tri-Motor:** Early commercial airliner; three engines for safety and reliability.
- **Boeing 247 (1933):** First modern all-metal airliner with retractable landing gear.
- **Douglas DC-2 (1934):** Set 19 speed and distance records; could fly coast-to-coast in a day.
- **Douglas DC-3 (1935):** First commercial airliner to generate a profit.
- **Boeing 307 Stratoliner:** First pressurized airliner, capable of flying in the stratosphere.

---

## **Research & Technological Advancements**

- **NACA (Langley, Virginia):** Wind-tunnel testing to reduce drag and improve performance.
- **Retractable Landing Gear:** Reduced parasitic drag.
- **Radio Beams:** Allowed for early instrument navigation.
- **Instrument Flying:** Enabled safe flight in low visibility.
- **Gyroscopic Instruments (Doolittle):** Foundation for autopilot and artificial horizon systems.
- **Wiley Post:** Conducted high-altitude flights and discovered the **jet stream**.

---

## **Airline Growth & Infrastructure**

- Post-WWI, airlines developed rapidly as technology advanced.
- Air travel evolved from luxury transport to commercial necessity.
- Example: Berlin → New York flight in ~25 hours (26 passengers).
- Airports developed with **lit runways, control towers, dispatchers, and stewardesses**.
- **Ellen Church:** First flight attendant (trained nurse).
- **1929:** Flags replaced by lights for night landings.
- **1938 (Pittsburgh):** First instrument landing system installed.
- **1930s:** Cleveland Tower inspired standardized airport control towers.

---

## **Airspace & Regulation**

- **1919 Paris Convention:** Gave each country sovereignty over its own airspace.
- **1926 Air Commerce Act:**
    - Created an Aeronautics Branch under the Department of Commerce.
    - Required pilot and mechanic licensing, medical exams, and aircraft certification.
    - Prohibited single-engine night operations.
- **CAA → FAA:** Oversaw civil aviation regulation.
- **CAB → NTSB:** Accident investigation responsibility shifted over time.
- **ICAO (1944 Chicago Convention):** Established to unify international aviation standards.

---

## **Aviation Culture**

- Early image: **luxury travel** with well-dressed passengers and onboard service.
- **Hindenburg Disaster (1937):** Ended public confidence in airships as passenger transport.
- **Eisenhower Highway System (1956):** Influenced by WWII logistics, improving airfield access.

---

## **Military Aviation: Interwar Period**

- Innovation despite the **Treaty of Versailles** restricting German rearmament.
- **Billy Mitchell:**
    - Youngest U.S. general, WWI hero (St. Mihiel offensive).
    - Advocated for an indepenbv op,dent Air Force.
    - Demonstrated air power by sinking the *Ostfriesland* (1921).
- Rise of the **German Luftwaffe** through covert rebuilding and training.

---

## **Jimmy Doolittle (1896 – 1993)**

- **WWI:** Flight instructor and gunnery trainer; helped demobilize aviation units.
- **1920s:** Studied at MIT; earned master’s and doctorate in aeronautical engineering.
- Won multiple air races (including McKay Trophy).
- Managed **Shell Oil’s aviation division**; developed **100-octane fuel**.
- Invented **gyroscope**based **attitude indicator** and **autopilot**.
- **1932:** First “blind” flight—took off, flew, and landed solely using instruments.
- **WWII:**
    - Led the **Doolittle Raid** (April 1942) — first U.S. air attack on Japan after Pearl Harbor.
    - Awarded the **Medal of Honor** for leadership and heroism.
    - Returned to active duty as an Air Corps Major.

---

# 🌍 **World War II (1939 – 1945)**

## **Outbreak of War**

- **Sept 1, 1939:** Germany invaded Poland → start of WWII.
- **Blitzkrieg Tactics:** Combined fast armor and air assaults (BF-109s, Ju-87 Stukas).
- **Evacuation at Dunkirk:** British Army escaped German encirclement.

---

## **Battle of Britain (1940–1941)**

- **Aug 1940 – May 1941:** German Luftwaffe vs. RAF.
- **RAF Commander:** Hugh Dowding; implemented radar, IFF (Identification Friend or Foe), and centralized command.
- Aircraft: **Spitfires** and **Hurricanes.**
- Casualties: 544 RAF pilots killed; ~2,700 Luftwaffe losses.
- Outcome: Britain survived; Germany turned toward the Eastern Front.
- **Norden Bomb Sight:** Increased bombing accuracy.

---

## **Pearl Harbor & Pacific War**

- **Dec 7, 1941:** Japan attacked Pearl Harbor — “Tora! Tora! Tora!”
- **April 1942:** Doolittle Raid on Tokyo (retaliatory bombing).
- **Flying Tigers:** Volunteer U.S. pilots aiding China before Pearl Harbor.

---

## **Battle of Midway (June 4, 1942)**

- U.S. codebreakers intercepted Japanese plans.
- U.S. carriers **Enterprise**, **Yorktown**, and **Hornet** attacked Japanese carriers **Akagi** and **Kaga**.
- Turning point in the Pacific — Japan lost four carriers.

---

## **WWII Aviation Expansion**

- **Training:** Massive programs for pilots, bombardiers, radio operators, and mechanics.
- **Production:** Over 100,000 aircraft produced per year.
- **Technology:**
    - Radar and transponder interrogation systems.
    - Electronic jamming and countermeasures.
    - Improved navigation and communication systems.

---

## **End of WWII**

- **Aug 1945:** Atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki.
- **Sept 2, 1945:** Japan formally surrendered aboard the *USS Missouri*.
