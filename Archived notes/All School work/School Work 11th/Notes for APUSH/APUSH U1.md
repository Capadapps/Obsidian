### Pre-Columbus Tribes

- **Hohokam**: Largest irrigation systems through canals. Crops such as maize were kept fertile by river canals. Located in southern Arizona. They had a social hierarchy and lived in compounds.
- **Acoma Pueblo (Journey of Coronado)**: Lived in financial and political disappointment due to their search for gold. Villages became central points for exploration. Culture and economy based on agriculture; they worshipped gods. Adapted to the desert of Arizona, farmed the three sisters with complex irrigation. Homes underground due to the heat.
- **Apache**: Oñate’s expedition was sponsored by the Spanish and driven by the motivation of the city of gold, Quivira, in the Great Plains. They occupied Arizona, New Mexico, west Texas, and northern Mexico. Semi-nomadic hunters; built homes from wood and teepees.
- **Shoshone**: Lived in the mountains of northwest Wyoming. The “Sheep Eaters” hunted for tools and food, impoverished with little to no horses. Profound spiritual connection to the land, resilience, and semi-nomadic hunter-gatherers.
- **Ute**: Resided in Utah and Colorado, groups of 20–100. Celebrated with dances, hunted wildlife, lived nomadically in teepees. Cultivated plants and foods. Later acquired horses from the Spanish and became respected warriors.
- **Lenape**: Settled in modern-day Philadelphia/Delaware region but relocated every 20 years due to soil depletion. Traded land with the Dutch and William Penn. Peaceful relationships but eventually pushed out. Hunted deer, women married young, made tools and homes.
- **Secotan Tribe**: Lived in huts in the Carolina region. Agriculture and water resources were central to the economy.
- **Iroquois**: Believed the Great Spirit ruled over all beings. Viewed humans and nature equally. Located in upstate New York.
- **Aleuts**: Vitus Bering expedition. Unangan people created sea vessels. Lived in the Aleutian Islands (between Alaska and Russia). Homes underground for warmth.
- **Tlingit Tribe**: Lived in southeastern Alaska panhandle. Ceremonies and food central to culture. Wood carving important. Ate wild celery in spring, clams in winter. Clan members inherited housing.

### Columbian Exchange

- **Potato**: Boosted food supply and reduced famine. Europe and the U.S. became dependent due to high yield and solid structure. Drove population growth and political shifts.
- **Silver Mines of Potosí**: Antonio Vázquez described Spanish colonies in Central/South America. The mountain near Potosí River (Bolivia) became unstable and overmined. Extremely dangerous; natives used as forced labor.
- **Caravel**: Small, fast sailing ship (15th–16th century). Used in Columbus’ voyage. Could sail against the wind; key innovation in exploration.
- **Magnetic Compass**: Columbus noticed magnetic variation (north vs. northwest). Provided reliable navigation, but when it failed morale dropped.
- **Crowd Diseases**: Colonists noted natives ravaged by diseases. No reverse spread into Europe. Smallpox was the deadliest, wiping out huge populations.
- **Smallpox Outbreak**: William Bradford, Plymouth governor, noted over 95% of natives died. Ruthless disease.
- **Cattle**: Gardner listed grievances about livestock destroying crops. Created conflicts between settlers and natives.
- **Horses**: Used in conquest to intimidate and massacre. Valuable asset in battle, changed warfare, improved mobility.
- **Wheat**: Fertile soils in the Americas fueled growth. High demand allowed exports to Europe, funding development. Supported the rise of the merchant class.
